# PUBLICATION POLICY

*What may be published as a LadybugBets analytical statement.*

This policy governs how any market observation is presented publicly. Its
purpose is to keep facts, calculations, and interpretations clearly separated
and attributable.

## The three layers of a published statement

Every published market statement must distinguish these three layers, and must
not silently conflate them:

1. **SOURCE FACT** — a value received from a governed data source, presented as
   observed and attributed to that source (e.g. "Source X showed price P for
   outcome O at time T").
2. **DERIVED CALCULATION** — a value LadybugBets computed from source facts,
   labeled as derived, with the method named (e.g. "implied probability under
   the 1/decimal method").
3. **INTERPRETATION** — commentary or framing about what the facts and
   calculations might mean, labeled as interpretation and stated with its
   uncertainty.

A reader must always be able to tell which layer any given number or claim
belongs to.

## Requirements for a governed publication

- **Timestamp** where temporally relevant — prices are time-sensitive.
- **Provenance** — the source, event, market, and outcome behind each observed
  value.
- **Reproducible calculations** where feasible — a derived value should be
  reproducible from the stated source facts and method.
- **Correction capability** — published statements must be correctable when an
  error is found.
- **No fabricated sources** — sources must be real and correctly attributed.
- **No invented historical data** — history must come from actual recorded
  observations.
- **Clear distinction between current and stale prices** — a price no longer
  current must be marked as such, not presented as live.

## Governing principle

> A number without provenance is not a governed LadybugBets market observation.

If a value cannot be attributed to its source, time, event, market, and outcome,
it must not be published as a LadybugBets market observation.
